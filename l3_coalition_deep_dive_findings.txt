L3 Coalition Deep Dive — Key Findings
======================================
Generated: 2026-02-22
Source: visuals/l3_coalition_deep_dive.py (1000 bootstrap samples, 5.12M coalition records)
Figures: data/analysis/figures/l3_deep_dive/


Overview
--------
The L3 Shapley/Banzhaf attributions are overwhelmingly driven by small (2-4 player)
restricted games. Marginal contributions drop 10-20x between coalition size s=0 and
s=3. By s=5, most agents are within 1% of zero. The specific subgames that matter
most involve mixtures of strong and weak agents, where the strong agent dramatically
restructures the equilibrium.


Agent Tiers (from Analysis 1: Marginal by Coalition Size)
---------------------------------------------------------
  Always-positive:   OpenAI-5.2-Low, PPO
                     Marginals stay positive at every coalition size. Never hurt the ecosystem.

  Small-only:        MAPPO, OpenAI-5.2, EF1, NFSP
                     Positive in small coalitions, near-zero in large ones.

  Sign-flippers:     PSRO
                     Positive for s<4, negative for s>4. Cancels under Banzhaf.

  Drags:             Walk, Tough, Soft
                     Negative at nearly every size except singleton.


The PSRO Puzzle — Solved (Analysis 5)
-------------------------------------
PSRO has 32% equilibrium weight but near-zero Banzhaf. The PPO vs PSRO size overlay
(a5_ppo_psro_size_overlay.png) makes the mechanism clear:

  - Both PPO and PSRO start at ~85% NW at s=0 (singleton).
  - PPO stays positive across ALL coalition sizes (+0.82% even at s=9).
  - PSRO crosses zero around s=4 and goes to -0.36% at s=8.

Banzhaf weights all sizes equally, so PSRO's positive and negative marginals cancel
to near-zero. Shapley upweights the extremes (s=0 and s=9 get weight 1/n), which
captures the large positive singleton value. PPO doesn't have this problem because
it never goes negative — both Shapley and Banzhaf are high.

The PSRO NW marginal histogram (a5_psro_nw_histogram.png) confirms: the distribution
is centered near zero with heavy tails in both directions.


Specific Subgames Driving Attributions (Analysis 2)
---------------------------------------------------

1. The {Soft} solo game is everyone's goldmine

   Agent joining {Soft}     NW marginal    NW+ marginal
   ─────────────────────    ───────────    ────────────
   OpenAI-5.2-Low            +29.3%         +74.5%
   PPO                       +21.2%            —
   PSRO                      +19.8%            —
   MAPPO                     +19.8%            —
   NFSP                      +17.2%         +26.9%
   OpenAI-5.2                +20.0%         +44.9%
   EF1                       +18.7%         +47.8% (from {Soft, Tough})

   Soft alone is so exploitable that any competent agent joining it gets massive
   credit. This single 2-player subgame is the #1 non-singleton contributor for
   almost every agent. Soft functions as an "inflatable target" — it inflates
   everyone else's attribution.


2. {Tough, NFSP} is the most toxic pair in the game

   Tough joining {NFSP} produces:
     -13.6% NW,  -54.8% NW+,  -70.0pp EF1

   This 2-player subgame is catastrophic. The NFSP-Tough equilibrium is deeply
   unfair and welfare-destroying. Interestingly, adding Walk partially buffers
   this: Tough joining {NFSP, Walk} = -8.5% NW (vs -13.6%). Walk as a stabilizer
   in this context is counterintuitive.


3. OpenAI-5.2-Low literally cannot hurt Nash Welfare

   Its worst NW coalition across all 512 subgames is +0.45%
   (joining {MAPPO, NFSP, PPO, PSRO, Walk}).

   No other agent has this property — universally beneficial for NW. Same for NW+:
   worst coalition is still +1.75%. The mechanism is that it's so strong it always
   improves the equilibrium by pulling weight toward itself.

   Similarly, OpenAI-5.2-Low's bottom-5 UW coalitions are all still positive
   (minimum +0.22%). It cannot reduce utilitarian welfare in any subgame.


4. PPO is a fairness chameleon — depends entirely on the subgame

   PPO joining {Soft, Tough}:   +50.6pp EF1   (huge fairness boost)
   PPO joining {EF1, PSRO}:     -34.7pp EF1   (huge fairness destroyer)

   Same agent, opposite effect. In weak-agent subgames, PPO restructures the
   equilibrium toward fairness. But when EF1 and PSRO already achieve fairness,
   PPO disrupts it. PPO's aggregate Banzhaf for EF1 lands somewhere in between
   because these effects partially cancel.


5. EF1 never hurts fairness — in any subgame, period

   EF1's minimum EF1 marginal across all 512 coalitions is +0.15pp
   (joining {MAPPO, NFSP, PPO, PSRO, Soft, Tough}).

   Even in a 7-player game packed with competitive RL agents, adding EF1 still
   improves fairness. Its top coalitions are dramatic: +100pp joining the empty
   coalition, +85.8pp joining {Tough}.

   But EF1's NW+ story is more nuanced — EF1 joining {OpenAI-5.2-Low} gives
   -12.7% NW+. Fairness and efficiency are in genuine tension for EF1 in
   strong-agent subgames.


6. Tough's NW+ redemption in the {PPO, Walk} subgame

   Tough joining {PPO, Walk}:  +7.6% NW+

   Tough alone has 0% NW+ (singleton advantage is zero). But in this specific
   3-player subgame, Tough creates positive advantages. The likely mechanism:
   Tough gives Walk someone else to compete with, changing the equilibrium
   structure so PPO doesn't crush Walk as completely, creating a more balanced
   game with positive NW+ for all.


7. Walk joining {OpenAI-5.2-Low} is the single most destructive entry

   -13.3% NW,  -52.8% NW+

   Walk entering a solo OpenAI-5.2-Low game destroys more welfare than any other
   2-player entry. Walk drags the equilibrium down just enough to waste
   OpenAI-5.2-Low's potential, but not enough to create any compensating structure.


Pairwise Synergy Patterns (Analysis 3)
---------------------------------------
Heatmaps: a3_synergy_{uw,nw,nw_plus,ef1}.png

Key patterns from the NW+ synergy matrix:

  Synergistic pairs (partner's presence INCREASES target's marginal):
    - Walk synergizes with PPO (+5.1), PSRO (+4.1), MAPPO (+4.2)
      Walk creates exploitable opponents; strong agents benefit from its presence.
    - Tough synergizes with PPO (+3.9), MAPPO (+2.5)
      Similar mechanism — Tough is exploitable but not as much as Walk.

  Antagonistic pairs (partner's presence DECREASES target's marginal):
    - OpenAI-5.2-Low is antagonistic with almost everyone (-5 to -10 column)
      It's so strong that when it's present, there's less room for others.
    - PPO and PSRO are mutually antagonistic (-6.1 NW+)
      They compete for the same equilibrium niche.
    - OpenAI-5.2 and PSRO are antagonistic (-6.1 NW+)
      Same niche competition.

  The synergy matrix is NOT symmetric: Walk helping PPO (+5.1) is not the same
  as PPO helping Walk (-0.40). The direction matters — weak agents help strong
  agents by being exploitable, but strong agents don't reciprocate.


Equilibrium Displacement (Analysis 4)
--------------------------------------
Heatmap: a4_equilibrium_displacement.png

Who steals whose equilibrium weight:

  PPO entering crushes:
    - NFSP: -0.28 weight (largest displacement in the matrix)
    - MAPPO: -0.19
    - PSRO: -0.18
    PPO directly steals equilibrium mass from these three agents.

  PSRO entering crushes:
    - NFSP: -0.14
    - MAPPO: -0.14
    Same victims, smaller magnitude — PSRO is a weaker version of PPO's displacement.

  OpenAI-5.2 entering crushes:
    - MAPPO: -0.24 (strongest single displacement)
    Surprisingly, OpenAI-5.2 displaces MAPPO more than PPO does.

  NFSP gains weight when EF1 enters (+0.11)
    EF1 changes the equilibrium landscape in NFSP's favor. This is likely because
    EF1 constrains the game toward fair outcomes, and NFSP happens to perform well
    under those constraints.

  Walk and Tough displace each other modestly (-0.01 to -0.02)
    The two weak agents compete for the same "bottom of the equilibrium" niche.


Summary: Which Restricted Games Matter Most
--------------------------------------------
1. The attributions are dominated by 2-3 player subgames.

2. The single most important subgame type is {strong agent joining Soft/Walk/Tough}.
   These inflate the strong agent's marginal because the weak agent is so exploitable.

3. The {Tough, NFSP} 2-player game is uniquely destructive and drags down
   both agents' attributions significantly.

4. OpenAI-5.2-Low's dominance comes from universality — it helps in EVERY subgame,
   not just specific ones. This is qualitatively different from PPO/MAPPO, which
   help in most but hurt in some.

5. PSRO's near-zero Banzhaf is not because it's unimportant — it has large
   positive AND negative marginals. The sign flip at coalition size ~4 causes
   exact cancellation under uniform (Banzhaf) weighting.

6. Fairness attribution is dominated by EF1's universal positive contribution
   and the strong negative effects of Walk/Tough in small coalitions. PPO's
   fairness contribution is highly context-dependent.
